DR: Daniel, here's what I've found. Your examination is reassuring: the pain is coming from the muscles and joints on the left side of your lower back, with some muscle spasm. Your nerves are all working normally — strength, reflexes and sensation in both legs are perfect, and the tests that would pick up a trapped nerve or disc pressing on a nerve are negative. There are no warning signs of anything serious. This is what we call a mechanical back strain, which is very common after lifting, and the good news is that most people recover well within 2 to 6 weeks.

About the MRI: I understand why you'd want one, but I'd advise against it right now, and here's why. Guidelines are clear that scans aren't helpful in the first 6 weeks unless there are warning signs, and you have none. Scans in people your age very often show disc bulges and wear-and-tear changes that are present in people with no pain at all — so they often cause worry and don't change treatment. A scan also wouldn't make you better faster. If you're not improving by about 6 weeks, or if anything changes, we'd absolutely reconsider.

The plan: the most important thing is to keep moving. Lying on the couch actually slows recovery. Walk regularly, several short walks a day, and gradually do more as the pain allows — it's safe to move even if it hurts a bit. Heat packs are fine. I'd like you to take regular paracetamol, 1 gram up to 4 times a day, and add an anti-inflammatory, ibuprofen 400 mg three times a day with food, for about 5 days. Because of your old stomach ulcer, I'll give you a stomach-protecting tablet to take alongside it, and stop the ibuprofen if you get any stomach pain or black stools. We'll avoid stronger painkillers like opioids — they don't work well for back pain and have side effects. I'll also refer you to a physiotherapist to guide your return to activity, and I can give you a certificate for modified work — ideally keep working, but set up a proper desk, stand and move every 30 minutes, and avoid heavy lifting for a couple of weeks.
