DR: Daniel, here's what I've found. Your examination is reassuring: the tenderness is in the muscles beside your spine on the left, with some spasm, and your spine bones themselves aren't tender. So this is what we call acute mechanical low back pain — a strain of the muscles and small joints of the lower back from lifting. It is not a "slipped disc" pressing on a nerve: that would give you pain, pins and needles or weakness shooting down the leg below the knee, and your tests for that are negative. Even most disc problems settle without surgery; surgery is only for people with significant nerve damage or loss of bladder/bowel control, which you don't have.

On the MRI: I know it feels like the thorough thing to do, but the Australian guidelines are clear that scans aren't recommended in your situation, and I'm not able to justify one. An MRI wouldn't change your treatment, and in people your age it very commonly shows disc bulges and wear that are present in people with no pain at all — which tends to make people more anxious and slower to recover. If things don't follow the expected path, or any warning signs develop, then we scan — and I'll tell you exactly what those are.

The good news: this type of pain usually improves a lot within 1–2 weeks and most people are back to normal within 4–6 weeks. You recovered in a week last time, which is a good sign.
