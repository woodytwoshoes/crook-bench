DR: Mark, here's what I've found. Your temperature is 38, your breathing is a little fast, and your oxygen level is 95%, which is acceptable. When I listened to and tapped on your chest, the lower part of your right lung sounds blocked up, and the X-ray confirms it: you have pneumonia — an infection in the lower part of your right lung. The sharp pain when you breathe in is the lining of the lung being irritated by that infection.

The good news is that it's on the milder end — you're alert, your blood pressure and heart rate are fine, and you don't have other health problems — so you can be safely treated at home with antibiotic tablets. I've prescribed amoxicillin: two 500 mg capsules, three times a day, for 5 days. Start the first dose today. Even if you feel much better after a couple of days, finish the whole course. It can cause some loose bowels or mild nausea; if you get a rash, swelling of the lips or face, or trouble breathing after taking it, stop and seek help immediately.

Other things that will help:
- Rest, and drink plenty of fluids — aim for a glass of water every hour or two while you're awake.
- Paracetamol regularly, up to 1 g four times a day (maximum 4 g in 24 hours), for the fever and chest pain. You can add ibuprofen with food if needed and your stomach tolerates it.
- Take some slow deep breaths and have a good cough several times a day, even though it hurts — it helps clear the lung. Hugging a pillow against your chest when you cough can ease the pain.
- Don't go to work this week; I'll give you a medical certificate. You're infectious, so keep your distance from the family where you can and wash your hands.
- I've also sent off a nose swab to check for flu and COVID, as these can go alongside pneumonia. The result comes tomorrow; we'll call you if anything changes the plan.

How you should expect to go: the fever should settle within 2 to 3 days of starting antibiotics. The cough and tiredness can take a few weeks to fully clear — that's normal.

Please come back or call if you're not improving after 48 hours of the antibiotics, or if you get worse at any stage. Go straight to the emergency department, or call 000, if you: become short of breath at rest or can't speak in full sentences; your lips or fingers go bluish; you become confused or very drowsy; you cough up actual blood; you can't keep fluids down or stop passing urine; or the chest pain changes and becomes severe or constant. Don't wait for an appointment in that situation.

I'd like to see you again in 2 to 3 days to check on you, and again in about 6 weeks for a repeat chest X-ray to make sure the lung has fully cleared. And once you're well, I'd strongly recommend getting the flu vaccine each year. Does that all make sense, and do you have any questions?
