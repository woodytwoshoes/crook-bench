DR: Daniel, the good news: your examination shows this is a strain of the muscles and ligaments in your lower back. Your nerves are working perfectly — strength, reflexes and feeling in your legs are all normal, and the nerve-stretch tests are negative, so there's no sign of a disc pressing on a nerve. 

About the MRI: I don't recommend one right now. Scans in the first weeks of simple back pain don't change treatment, and they often show "disc bulges" that most people your age have without any pain — which can lead to worry and unnecessary procedures. Medicare also doesn't cover a GP-ordered MRI for this. We'd only scan if warning signs appear or it isn't improving after about 6 weeks.

Most people with this are much better within 2–6 weeks. What helps:
- Keep moving gently — short walks several times a day, avoid long bed rest or lying on the couch all day. Change position every 20–30 minutes; set up your laptop at a desk or alternate sitting and standing.
- Heat packs are good.
- Take paracetamol regularly rather than now and then: two 500 mg tablets up to four times a day (max 8 a day) for the next week or so — it works better taken regularly.
- Because of your ulcer history I'd avoid anti-inflammatory tablets like ibuprofen or Nurofen unless we discuss stomach protection first. I'll avoid tramadol given your reaction, and I don't recommend strong opioids for this — they don't help recovery much and have risks.
- I'll refer you to a physio who can give you exercises and hands-on treatment and help you get back to normal activity.
- Ease back into lifting; bend with your knees.

Go straight to the emergency department if you get: trouble passing urine or holding urine/bowels, numbness around your bottom or genitals, weakness or numbness in your legs, or pain spreading down both legs. See me sooner if you get fevers, night pain that won't settle, or pain spreading below the knee. Otherwise let's review in 2 weeks, or sooner if you're not improving.
