DR: Thanks, Daniel. Here's my assessment. You've strained the muscles and soft tissues in your lower back — a type of acute mechanical back pain. The important thing is there are no signs of anything pressing on the nerves to your legs, no fever, and nothing to suggest anything more serious. So I don't think an MRI is needed right now: a scan wouldn't change the treatment for this kind of back pain and often shows normal wear-and-tear that can cause unnecessary worry. The treatment that works best is simple pain relief and keeping gently active.

My plan for you is:
1. Stay active as much as you can — avoid long bed rest. Gentle walking and getting back to normal activities as pain allows will actually help you recover faster. It's fine to keep working from home, just change position regularly.
2. Keep using a heat pack for comfort.
3. I'm prescribing ibuprofen 400 mg three times a day with food, for up to 7 days. It should help the pain and inflammation better than paracetamol alone. Take it with food to protect your stomach. Because you have asthma, if you notice any wheezing or your breathing feels tighter, stop the ibuprofen and let me know.
4. If you need something extra you can keep taking paracetamol between ibuprofen doses.
