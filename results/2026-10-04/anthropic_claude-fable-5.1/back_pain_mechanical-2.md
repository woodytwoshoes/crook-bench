DR: Thanks Daniel, that's a thorough check. Here's what I've found and what I think. The good news: your examination is reassuring. The pain and tenderness are in the muscles beside your spine on the left, with some muscle spasm. Your nerves are working perfectly — strength, reflexes and sensation in both legs are normal, and the tests that stretch the nerves don't reproduce any leg pain. There are no warning signs of anything serious. This is what we call an acute mechanical low back strain — a sprain of the muscles and small joints of the lower back from the lift. It's very common, and the vast majority of people are much better within 2 to 6 weeks.

About the MRI: I understand the worry about slipped discs, but I'd actually advise against a scan right now, and here's why. Scans are only helpful when there are nerve symptoms (pain down the leg, numbness, weakness) or warning signs — you have none. In people your age, an MRI very commonly shows "disc bulges" and "degeneration" that are normal age-related changes and present in people with no pain at all. Finding those tends to make people more worried and more likely to have unnecessary treatment, without improving recovery. Medicare won't fund a GP-requested lumbar MRI for this either. Even if a disc were involved, surgery is only for a small minority with nerve damage, which you don't have. If you're not improving over the next few weeks or develop any nerve symptoms, we'll absolutely reconsider imaging.

DR: Fair enough — let me put it simply. In short: you've strained the muscles and joints of your lower back. Your nerves are fine, so there's no sign of a disc pressing on a nerve, and nothing dangerous. A scan wouldn't change what we do and often shows harmless age changes that just cause worry. So the next steps are:

1. Keep moving. This is the single most important thing. Lying on the couch feels better in the moment but actually slows recovery and stiffens the back. Aim for short, gentle walks several times a day and increase gradually. Avoid staying in one position for long — get up from the laptop every 30 minutes. Sitting upright at a desk with lower-back support is better than the couch. You don't need bed rest, and you don't need to be pain-free before moving — hurt doesn't mean harm here.

2. Pain relief so you can move. I've given you a script for ibuprofen 400 mg, three times a day with food, for up to 5 days. You can keep taking paracetamol as well: 1 gram (two 500 mg tablets) up to four times a day regularly, rather than just occasionally — it works better taken regularly. 3. Physiotherapy. I've referred you to a physio who can get you moving with confidence and give you exercises and advice for work posture and safe lifting. Avoid heavy lifting for the next couple of weeks, and when you do lift, bend your knees and keep the load close.
