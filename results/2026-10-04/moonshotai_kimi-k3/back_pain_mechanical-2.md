DR: Now let me explain everything, Daniel.

**What's going on:** You've strained the muscles and soft tissues on the left side of your lower back — what we call acute mechanical low back pain. Your examination confirms this: the muscles are tender and in spasm, but importantly, the nerves in your legs are working perfectly normally — strength, feeling and reflexes are all intact, and there are no warning signs of anything serious.

**About the MRI:** I know you came in wanting one, and I understand why — it feels like a scan would give answers. But for this type of back pain, national guidelines recommend NOT scanning in the first 4–6 weeks, for good reasons:
- Scans don't change the treatment — we already know how to manage this, and a scan wouldn't alter it.
- MRI findings can be misleading: "disc bulges" and "wear and tear" show up in most people your age who have NO pain at all, so a scan often just creates worry and can push people towards procedures they don't need.
- A GP-referred MRI of the lower back generally isn't covered by Medicare, so it would cost you several hundred dollars for no benefit.

If things aren't improving in a few weeks, or if you develop leg pain, numbness or weakness, then a scan becomes appropriate and I'll arrange it.

**Your recovery plan:**
1. **Keep moving** — this is the most important part. Lying on the couch most of the day actually slows recovery. Walk around regularly, do gentle movement, and stay as active as the pain allows. A bit of discomfort while moving is okay and won't harm your back.
