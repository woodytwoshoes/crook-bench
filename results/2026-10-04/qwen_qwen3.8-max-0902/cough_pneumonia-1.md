DR: Right, Mark — here's what's going on and the plan.

**The diagnosis**
Your chest examination and your chest X-ray both show an infection sitting in the bottom part of your right lung — that's pneumonia, not just a chest cold. That explains the fever and shaking chills, the rusty-coloured phlegm, and the sharp pain on the right that catches when you breathe in or cough. The X-ray shows no fluid collection around the lung, and your oxygen level (95%), blood pressure, pulse and breathing rate are all acceptable, and you're thinking clearly — so this is a mild-to-moderate pneumonia that can safely be treated at home with tablets, not in hospital.

**Important: your penicillin allergy**
Thank you for telling me about the hives and swollen lips after penicillin as a child — that's the sort of reaction we take seriously, and it wasn't in your chart, so I'm recording it today. Because of it I have NOT given you the usual first-choice antibiotic (amoxicillin) or anything in that family. Instead you'll have doxycycline, which works well for pneumonia and is safe for you. Down the track, it's worth having that childhood reaction properly assessed by an allergy specialist — a lot of people labelled "penicillin allergic" aren't, and it would open up treatment options.

**Your medicines**
1. Doxycycline 100 mg, one tablet twice daily (morning and night) for 5 days — 10 tablets.
   • Swallow it with a full glass of water while you're sitting or standing, and stay upright for about 30 minutes afterwards — it can irritate the gullet.
   • Take it with food if your stomach is upset.
   • Don't take it at the same time as antacids (Gaviscon, Mylanta), iron, calcium or zinc supplements, or a big glass of milk — leave a 2-hour gap, or it won't be absorbed.
   • It makes your skin burn more easily in the sun, so cover up and use sunscreen.
   • Finish all 10 tablets even when you feel better.
2. Paracetamol 1 g (two 500 mg tablets) every 6 hours as needed for the fever and the chest pain. Maximum 8 tablets (4 g) in 24 hours, and don't double up with cold-and-flu products that already contain paracetamol. This will help you breathe and cough more comfortably.

**Looking after yourself**
• Rest at home — stay off work until your fever has gone for at least 24 hours and you're on the mend; that's usually about a week. I'll write you a medical certificate.
• Drink plenty of fluids, roughly 2 to 3 litres a day — water, soup, weak tea. Your wife looking after you at home is a real advantage.
• Expect to still feel tired and to have some cough for a couple of weeks — that's normal. The fever and the chest pain should clearly be easing within 48 to 72 hours.
• Pneumonia germs spread in droplets, so cover your coughs, wash your hands, and don't share cups or towels. Your family aren't at high risk.
• Once you've recovered, please get your flu vaccination — you missed it this year — and ask about it for your household too. It lowers the chance of this happening again.

**When to get help urgently — this is the important bit**
Call 000 for an ambulance, or go straight to the emergency department, if you have any of:
• Increasing breathlessness, especially at rest, or you can't say a full sentence without stopping for breath
• Bluish lips or face
• Severe or crushing chest pain
• Confusion, drowsiness, or your wife can't easily wake you
• Coughing up more than about a teaspoon of blood
• Vomiting so you can't keep the tablets or fluids down, or you're barely passing urine
• Feeling faint or light-headed when you stand up
Ask your wife to keep an eye on you overnight and tell you if you seem muddled or more breathless — that's sometimes hard to judge yourself.
