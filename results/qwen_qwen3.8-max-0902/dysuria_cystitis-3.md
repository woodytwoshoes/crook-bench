TREATMENT
I've prescribed nitrofurantoin 50 mg, one capsule four times a day for 5 days — take each dose with food or milk, which helps absorption and reduces stomach upset. I chose this rather than amoxicillin (what you had before) because the common bladder germ is now often resistant to amoxicillin, and nitrofurantoin works well and doesn't disturb your body's other bacteria as much. Finish the whole course even if you feel better after a day or two. It's normal for your urine to turn bright yellow while you're on it.
For the burning and ache you can take paracetamol — 1 or 2 tablets (up to 1 g), no more than 4 doses in 24 hours — and drink plenty of water.
The pill still works with these antibiotics. Only if you vomit or have severe diarrhoea would you need extra precautions.

TESTS AND FOLLOW-UP
Because this is your third UTI in 12 months, I've sent your urine to the lab for culture and sensitivity. That tells us exactly which germ it is and which antibiotics it responds to, so future episodes can be treated precisely instead of guessing. REDUCING THE EPISODES (this is important, since yours follow sex)
• Drink enough that your urine is pale — aim for about 2 litres of water a day; coffee counts less and can irritate the bladder, so cut back a bit.
• Pass urine as soon as you feel the urge, and try to wee straight after sex.
• Avoid douches, feminine washes, bubble baths and spermicide — you already avoid these, which is good.
• Wipe front to back, and avoid constipation.
• Cranberry products and D-mannose have only weak evidence; they're safe to try but not a substitute for the steps above.
If you keep getting infections despite this, we have further options: a single antibiotic dose taken after sex, a nightly preventive antibiotic for a few months, or a non-antibiotic urinary antiseptic called methenamine. At this stage you don't need scans of your kidneys or bladder; they're only useful if something unusual turns up, like persistent blood in the urine or an unusual germ.
