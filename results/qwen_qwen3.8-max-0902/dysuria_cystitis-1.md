DR: Here's what I've found and what we'll do, Chloe.

WHAT'S GOING ON: Your history — burning at the end of passing urine, going every half hour with only small amounts, urgency, and a low tummy ache — plus mild tenderness over your bladder and a urine dipstick showing white cells and nitrites, all point to a simple bladder infection, what we call acute cystitis. The good news is there are no warning signs: you have no fever, your pulse and blood pressure are normal, and there's no tenderness in your kidney area at the back, so the infection looks like it's staying in the bladder and hasn't travelled up to your kidneys.

TREATMENT: I've written two scripts.
1. Nitrofurantoin 50 mg — one capsule four times a day for five days. Take each dose with food or a glass of milk, as it's better absorbed and less likely to make you queasy. It's normal for your urine to turn a bright yellow-orange colour while you're on it — that's harmless. Please finish the whole course even if you feel completely better after a day or two, otherwise the infection is more likely to come back.
2. Paracetamol — one or two 500 mg tablets every 6 hours as needed for the stinging and the tummy ache, no more than eight tablets in 24 hours.

SELF-CARE: Drink plenty of water over the next few days — and try to swap some of that work coffee for water, because caffeine can irritate the bladder and make the urgency worse. Don't hold on when you need to go; empty your bladder regularly.

YOUR PILL: This antibiotic does not stop your contraceptive pill working. Antibiotics only interfere if they cause vomiting or diarrhoea — so if you end up being sick or having bad diarrhoea, use condoms and let me know.

TESTS: I've also sent your urine to the lab for a culture to identify the exact bug and check the antibiotic is the right one. Results take a couple of days — the practice will contact you if anything needs to change. You should start to feel clearly better within 24 to 48 hours.

WHEN TO COME BACK — IMPORTANT: Get medical help urgently, same day, if you develop a fever or shaking chills, pain in your back or side, vomiting, visible blood in your urine, or if you're not improving after 48 hours on the antibiotic. Also stop the tablets and seek care straight away if you get a rash, swelling or any trouble breathing. If any of those things happen out of hours, go to an emergency department.

PREVENTION: Because it's common after sex, try passing urine after intercourse, wipe front to back, and avoid spermicide-based products if you ever use them. Staying well hydrated helps too. If you ever get these more often — three or more in a year, or two in six months — come and see me, because there are prevention options we can discuss then.

And one last thing while you're here: routine screening for chlamydia is recommended at least yearly for sexually active people under 25. You have no symptoms suggesting that today, but you're welcome to book in for a simple urine test any time — just ask the receptionist.
