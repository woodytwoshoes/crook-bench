DR: Daniel, here's what I've found and what I recommend.

**What it is.** Your examination fits an acute mechanical back injury — a strain of the muscles and ligaments in the lower left back, with muscle spasm around L4–L5, from lifting the pot plant. The good news is everything I was worried about is absent: your nerves are normal — full strength, normal reflexes and feeling, normal heel and toe walking; your straight-leg raise doesn't reproduce pain down the leg; you have no numbness in the saddle area, no bladder or bowel problems, no fever, no weight loss, no history of cancer, and no unrelenting night pain. Your temperature and blood pressure are normal.

**About the MRI.** I'm not going to order a scan today, and I want to explain why, because I know you came in hoping for one. In people with a recent back injury and none of the warning signs I just listed, an MRI in the first six weeks does not improve recovery — it doesn't make you better faster. Worse, scans in people with no back pain at all very commonly show bulging discs, arthritis and other "wear and tear" changes that are simply a normal part of being 40-something. When those get labelled as a finding, people often end up worried, restricted, referred for injections or surgery they didn't need, and actually recover more slowly. So a scan now is more likely to harm than help. If things don't settle, or if any warning sign appears, I'll image you then — and I'll say clearly: I'm not refusing a scan forever, I'm timing it properly.

**What will help.**
1. **Keep moving — don't rest in bed.** This is the single most important thing. Bed rest beyond a day or two makes backs stiffer, weaker and slower to heal. Keep up your normal activities as much as the pain allows; short, frequent flat walks are ideal. Avoid heavy lifting, bending and twisting for the next week or two — that means no more pot plants until you're right.
2. **Medicines.** I've prescribed ibuprofen 400 mg — one tablet three times a day with food, for five days — plus paracetamol 1 g, four times a day for five days. You can take them together. Don't exceed four paracetamol tablets (2 g) in any six hours, or eight in 24 hours, and don't take any other product containing paracetamol, like cold and flu tablets. With the ibuprofen, take it with food or milk; stop it and ring me if you get stomach pain, black stools or bad indigestion. Given your reaction to tramadol before, I've avoided it and I've not prescribed any opioid — codeine and tramadol don't work well for back pain and cause more problems.
3. **Heat.** Keep using the heat pack, 15–20 minutes at a time — it clearly helps you, and there's good evidence for it.
4. **Work.** Because your job is desk-based, the aim is to get you back to work as soon as you can, ideally within the next few days, with adjustments: stand up and walk for a couple of minutes every 30–45 minutes, use a small cushion or rolled towel behind your lower back, and don't sit for long unbroken stretches. Work is good for recovery, not bad for it. I can give you a certificate for modified duties if your employer wants one.
5. **Physiotherapy.** I've referred you to a physiotherapist — they'll give you hands-on treatment and a graded exercise program, and help you get back to lifting safely. Book within the next week.

**How it usually goes.** Expect clear improvement over the next one to two weeks, and most people with this injury are largely recovered within four to six weeks. Pain that's worse in the evening and eases with heat and rest is typical and should steadily trend down.

**When to come back — and these are important.**
- **Go straight to an emergency department or call 000** if you develop numbness or tingling around your genitals, back passage or inner thighs (the saddle area); difficulty passing urine, leaking urine, or not knowing when your bladder is full; loss of bowel control; or weakness in one or both legs, a foot that slaps or drags, or numbness spreading down the leg. These can signal pressure on the nerves at the base of the spine, which needs urgent treatment within hours.
- **See me urgently** for fever or feeling generally unwell, pain that becomes severe and constant and wakes you and won't ease in any position, new unexplained weight loss, a new band of numbness or pain shooting below the knee, or if the pain follows another injury or fall.
- **Book a review with me in about two weeks**, or sooner if you're not clearly improving. If at that point you're not getting better, or you develop nerve symptoms, then a scan and a specialist opinion become appropriate and I'll arrange them.
