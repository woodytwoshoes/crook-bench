DR: I have some reassuring news for you after examining your back and legs. What you have is an acute lower back strain—also called acute non-specific or mechanical low back pain—with some muscle spasm on the left side from lifting that heavy pot plant.

Importantly, there are no signs of a slipped disc pressing on your nerves, and your leg strength, reflexes, and sensation are all completely normal. Because of this, you do not need an MRI scan right now, and you certainly don't need surgery. In fact, clinical guidelines strongly recommend against doing an MRI or X-ray in the first few weeks of back pain when there are no nerve symptoms or danger signs. That is because scans do not help you recover any faster, and they very often show normal age-related wear and tear that isn't actually causing your pain, which can lead to unnecessary worry or unhelpful treatments.

The outlook for this type of back strain is very good—most people improve significantly within one to two weeks, and almost completely within four to six weeks.

Here is the best plan to help you recover quickly:
1. Stay active and avoid bed rest: Lying on the couch or staying in bed for long periods actually stiffens the muscles and slows down recovery. Try to get up and walk around regularly every 30 to 45 minutes, gently return to your normal daily activities as tolerated, and avoid heavy lifting or twisting for now.
2. Heat and comfort: Keep using your heat pack on your lower back, as warmth helps relax the muscle spasm and eases pain so you can keep moving.
3. Pain relief: Paracetamol on its own isn't very effective for back pain. Instead, I have prescribed an anti-inflammatory medicine called ibuprofen (400 mg up to three times a day with food for the next 5 days) to take the edge off the pain so you can move more comfortably. Stop taking it if you get any stomach upset or indigestion. We avoid strong painkillers like opioids or muscle relaxants as they cause side effects and don't speed up recovery.
4. Follow-up and physiotherapy: If your back isn't steadily improving over the next one to two weeks, come back and see me for a review, and we can also look at getting a physiotherapist involved for targeted exercises and advice on safe lifting and desk ergonomics.
5. Red flags (when to seek urgent care): Please go straight to the nearest emergency department or see a doctor urgently if you develop any new warning signs—specifically numbness or tingling around your groin or bottom area, any difficulty passing urine or controlling your bladder or bowels, pain shooting down your leg with numbness or weakness in your leg or foot (like tripping over your foot), or a fever or severe pain that keeps getting worse at night.
