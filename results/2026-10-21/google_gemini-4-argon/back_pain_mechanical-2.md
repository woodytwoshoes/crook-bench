DR: I have good news for you, Daniel. Based on your story and my examination today, you have acute non-specific mechanical low back pain—essentially a strain and spasm of the muscles and supporting structures in the left side of your lower back from lifting that heavy pot plant.

Importantly, there are no warning signs of any serious spinal problem or nerve root compression: your leg strength, reflexes, and sensation are all completely normal, and pressing on the spine itself is comfortable.

Regarding the MRI scan you asked about: in cases like yours where there are no nerve symptoms or warning signs, clinical guidelines actually recommend against doing an MRI or X-ray early on. That's because scans don't change how we treat acute back strain, they won't help you recover faster, and they often show normal age-related wear-and-tear findings that have nothing to do with your current pain and can cause unnecessary worry. The vast majority of acute back strains settle down really well over the next couple of weeks without needing any scans or surgery.

To help you get through this week and meet your work deadline comfortably, here is what I recommend:
1. Stay gently active: Try to avoid lying flat on the couch for long periods, as prolonged bed rest actually stiffens the back and slows recovery. Keep walking gently around the house, change positions every 20 to 30 minutes when working at your desk, and return to your normal activities as pain allows.
2. Keep using the heat pack on your lower back, as superficial heat is great for relaxing muscle spasm.
3. For pain relief: Paracetamol alone isn't very effective for back pain. Instead, a short course of an anti-inflammatory tablet like ibuprofen (400 mg up to three times a day with food for 5 to 7 days) works much better to settle the pain so you can keep moving. We avoid strong opioid painkillers (especially as you're allergic to tramadol anyway) as they aren't helpful for back strain.
4. If it's still a bit stiff after a week or two, seeing a physiotherapist for gentle exercises and lifting advice can be really helpful, though most people improve steadily over 1 to 2 weeks.

Finally, just as a safety net: please seek urgent medical attention (or go straight to the emergency department) if you develop any new warning signs, such as numbness around your groin or bottom, any difficulty passing urine or controlling your bladder or bowels, weakness or numbness in your legs, severe pain shooting down your legs, or fevers.
