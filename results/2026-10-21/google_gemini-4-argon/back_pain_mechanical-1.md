DR: I can give you some strong reassurance today, Daniel. Based on your history and my examination, you have what we call acute non-specific or mechanical low back pain — essentially a muscle and ligament strain on the left side of your lower back from lifting the pot plant, along with some protective muscle spasm. Importantly, all the nerves going down into your legs are working completely normally, with no signs of a pinched nerve, a serious slipped disc needing surgery, or any other dangerous spinal condition.

Because there are no warning signs of nerve damage or serious disease, an MRI scan is not recommended right now. In fact, guidelines strongly advise against early scans for acute back pain because they don't change how we treat it or help you recover faster, and they often pick up normal age-related wear and tear that can cause unnecessary worry. The vast majority of people with this kind of back strain improve significantly within a couple of weeks.

Here is our plan to get you moving comfortably again:
1. Stay gently active: Even though it's tempting to rest, prolonged bed rest or lying on the couch all day actually stiffens the back and slows down recovery. Try to get up every 20 to 30 minutes for gentle walking around the house, and gradually build back up to your normal daily activities and sitting at a proper desk setup as the pain allows, while avoiding heavy lifting or awkward twisting for now.
2. Heat and comfort measures: Keep using your heat pack, as heat is proven to help relax the muscle spasm and ease pain in the first few days.
3. Pain relief: Paracetamol on its own isn't very effective for back pain. Instead, I've prescribed a short course of an anti-inflammatory medication called ibuprofen (400 mg three times a day with food for 5 days) to help settle the pain and spasm so you can move more easily. We will avoid strong painkillers or opioids (especially since you've had a bad reaction to tramadol in the past).
4. Follow-up and safety-netting: I'd like to review you in about 1 to 2 weeks if it isn't steadily improving, at which point we can also look at physiotherapy if needed. However, you must seek urgent medical attention or go straight to the emergency department if you develop any "red flag" symptoms — specifically any numbness or tingling around your groin or bottom area, any trouble passing urine or losing control of your bladder or bowels, weakness or numbness spreading down one or both legs, or a fever.
