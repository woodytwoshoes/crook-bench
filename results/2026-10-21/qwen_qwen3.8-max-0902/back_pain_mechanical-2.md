DR: Here's what I think is going on, Daniel, and my plan.

**The diagnosis:** You've strained the muscles and soft tissues in the left side of your lower back lifting that pot plant. On examination the tenderness is in the muscle beside the spine, not on the bone, your leg nerves are all working normally — strength, reflexes, feeling, heel and toe walking — and the nerve stretch tests don't reproduce any leg pain. So this is acute, non-specific mechanical back pain, not a "slipped disc" pressing on a nerve.

**Why I'm not sending you for an MRI today:** In the first few weeks, a scan doesn't change the treatment when there are no warning signs — and I've checked for all of them. More importantly, MRI picks up disc bulges and wear-and-tear in a huge number of people who have absolutely no back pain, so it often finds something that isn't the cause of your pain. That leads to worry, more tests, injections and sometimes surgery that people didn't need, and studies show those patients actually do worse. Your chance of getting better is very good: most people improve substantially within two weeks and mostly settle over four to six weeks, whether or not they have a scan. If you're still significantly sore at four to six weeks, or you develop nerve symptoms, I'll reconsider imaging then.

**Medicines:** I've prescribed ibuprofen 400 mg — one tablet three times a day with food, for five days. Take it regularly rather than waiting for the pain to peak. Stop it and ring me if you get stomach pain, indigestion, or black stools, and don't take any other anti-inflammatory like Nurofen or Voltaren on top of it. Alongside that, paracetamol: two 500 mg tablets four times a day — that's eight tablets in 24 hours, which is the absolute maximum, don't go over it. Your record shows tramadol upsets you, and I'm deliberately not giving you anything opioid-based like codeine or endone either; they don't work any better for back pain and they cause side effects and dependence.

**Keeping moving is the main treatment:** Lying on the couch all day actually slows recovery. Please get up and have several short, gentle walks a day — even five to ten minutes at a time — and build up. Keep working from home if you can, but change position every 20 to 30 minutes, sit with your lower back supported and your screen at eye level, and stand up and stretch regularly. Avoid lifting, bending and twisting — especially lifting something heavy out in front of you — until this settles. A hot pack or a warm shower on the sore area for 15 to 20 minutes helps a lot of people.

**Physiotherapy:** I've referred you to a physio. They can give you hands-on treatment, and more importantly a graded exercise and stretching program to get you moving normally again and help prevent this recurring — you had a similar twinge five years ago, so building up your back and core strength is worthwhile.
