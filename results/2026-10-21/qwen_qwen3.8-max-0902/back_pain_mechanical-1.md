About the MRI: I know you were expecting one, so let me be clear about why I'm not ordering it today. In the first four to six weeks, a scan doesn't change treatment and doesn't speed recovery — the management is the same whether we scan or not. MRI scans very often show age-related bulges and wear-and-tear in people who have no pain at all, and seeing those words on a report can make people more fearful and can lead to injections or surgery that aren't needed. I'd only arrange imaging urgently if there were warning signs of nerve or spinal cord compression, infection or a fracture — and you have none of those today. If you haven't clearly improved by around six weeks, or if you develop pain, numbness or weakness going down the leg, then I'll refer you for an MRI at that point. And even if a disc were bulging, the vast majority settle without surgery; surgery is reserved for progressive nerve damage or emergencies.

The plan:
1. Keep moving. This is the single most important thing. Lying on the couch with the laptop is actually slowing your recovery — bed rest makes backs stiffer and weaker. Aim for short, frequent walks around the house, and build up gradually. Avoid heavy lifting, bending and twisting for now, and use your legs rather than your back when you do lift.
2. Heat — a hot water bottle or heat pack on the sore area for 15–20 minutes several times a day helps spasm. A warm shower in the morning to loosen up is useful too.
