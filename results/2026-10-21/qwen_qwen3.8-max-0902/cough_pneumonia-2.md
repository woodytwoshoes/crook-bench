DR: Right, here's what's going on, Mark.

**What you have:** Your chest exam and the X-ray both show an infection in the lower part of your right lung — pneumonia. On the X-ray that area looks solid with air-filled tubes running through it, which is the classic picture. That explains the fever and shaking chills, the rusty-brown phlegm, the sharp pain on the right when you breathe in, and getting puffed walking from the car park. Your observations are reassuring though: oxygen level 95%, breathing 22 a minute, heart rate 96, blood pressure normal, and you're thinking clearly and eating and drinking normally. So this is a mild pneumonia and you can be treated at home — you don't need hospital.

**About antibiotics:** Yes, you do need them here, but not the usual first one. Standard treatment is amoxicillin, a penicillin — and you had hives and swollen lips with penicillin as a child, which is an allergic reaction we must take seriously. So I've prescribed doxycycline instead, from a completely different antibiotic family: one 100 mg tablet twice a day for five days, morning and evening. Take each one with a full glass of water while you're sitting or standing upright, and don't lie down for about half an hour afterwards, because it can irritate the gullet. Take it with some food. Keep milk, antacids like Gaviscon or Mylanta, and iron or calcium supplements about two hours apart from the dose, or it won't absorb. It can make your skin burn more easily in the sun, so cover up and use sunscreen. Finish all ten tablets even when you feel better — stopping early can let it come back. Please tell every doctor, dentist and pharmacist you see about the penicillin allergy; I'll record it clearly on your file here.

**For the fever and pain:** Paracetamol, two 500 mg tablets, up to four times a day as needed — that's a maximum of eight tablets in 24 hours, and never more often than every six hours. Don't take any other product that contains paracetamol, like cold-and-flu sachets, at the same time. Rest, and drink plenty of fluids — aim for pale urine. Your cough can hang around for a couple of weeks even after the infection has gone; that's normal.

**Time off work:** You'll need at least the rest of this week off school — I'd say five days, and until your fever has gone for 48 hours and you're feeling up to it. I'll write you a medical certificate. Teaching is a bit like a cough-magnifier, and you'd be running on empty.

**When to get help — this is the important bit:** Come back and see me, or get seen the same day, if you are not clearly improving within 48 hours, or if you get any of these: more breathless, or breathless while sitting still; chest pain that's getting worse; coughing up blood; fever still spiking after two days of tablets; lips or face looking blue; feeling confused, drowsy or muddled; not able to keep fluids down, or passing much less urine; a new rash, or swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, or wheezing after starting the antibiotic — that would be an allergic reaction, stop the tablet and call 000 straight away. If you can't get an appointment quickly in those situations, go to the emergency department. Your wife working from home is a real help — ask her to keep an eye out for you seeming drowsy or confused, since that's hard to spot yourself.

**Follow-up:** I want to see you again in two to three days to make sure you're turning the corner. And once you've recovered, please get your flu vaccination this year — you missed it this season and it would have lowered your chances of this. A repeat X-ray isn't usually needed for a never-smoker your age who recovers well, but if your cough or breathlessness is still there at six weeks we'll re-check it.

**Your family:** Pneumonia like this isn't caught as easily as a cold, but do cover your coughs and wash your hands; if your wife or the kids develop fever and a cough, they should be seen too. Nothing else needs to be done for them.
