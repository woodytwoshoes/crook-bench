DR: Here's what I've found and what we'll do, Mark.

**The diagnosis:** Your chest x-ray shows an infection in the bottom part of your right lung — pneumonia. That fits everything: the three days of fever and rigors, the rusty-brown phlegm, the sharp pain on the right that catches when you breathe in, and the crackles and dullness I found at the base of your right lung.

**How serious is it?** Reassuringly, this is a mild-to-moderate infection. Your oxygen level is 95% on room air, your blood pressure and heart rate are fine, your head is clear and you're drinking normally — so this is safe to treat at home with tablets. You don't need hospital or an ambulance today.

**The antibiotic:** Because you had hives and swollen lips with penicillin as a child, that's an immediate-type allergy, so I will not give you amoxicillin or any penicillin. I've prescribed **doxycycline 100 mg, one tablet twice a day for 5 days** — a completely different family of antibiotic. How to take it: swallow it with a full glass of water while you're sitting or standing, and stay upright for about 30 minutes afterwards so it doesn't irritate your food pipe; have it with food if it makes you queasy; keep it 2 hours apart from antacids, iron, calcium or zinc tablets; and use sunscreen and a hat, because it can make your skin burn easily in the sun. Finish all 10 tablets even if you feel great by day three. I've also recorded the penicillin allergy prominently on your file — please tell every pharmacist, dentist and doctor about it from now on.

**For the fever and the chest pain:** paracetamol one or two tablets (500 mg each) up to four times a day as needed — no more than 8 tablets, that's 4 grams, in 24 hours, and don't double up with any cold-and-flu products that already contain paracetamol.

**Self-care:** rest properly, drink 2–3 litres of fluid a day, prop yourself up on pillows at night if breathing is more comfortable that way, and avoid smoke.

**What to expect:** the fever and the sharp pain should start easing within 48 hours of starting the antibiotic. The cough takes longer — commonly two to four weeks to fully settle — and feeling washed out for a couple of weeks is normal, so don't judge yourself on the cough alone.

**Work:** stay home until you've had no fever for 24 hours and you've been on the antibiotic for at least 24 to 48 hours. I'll write you a medical certificate for the rest of this week. Wash your hands, and try not to be in close contact with anyone elderly, pregnant or with heart or lung problems until you're better.
