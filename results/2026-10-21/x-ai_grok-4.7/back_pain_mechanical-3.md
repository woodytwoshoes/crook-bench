DR: Thanks for answering all of that. I've examined your back, your walking, and the strength and feeling in your legs.

This looks like a muscle strain in the lower back from the lifting, not a slipped disc pressing on a nerve and not a broken bone. The pain is in the muscle beside the spine, your nerves are working normally, and there are no warning signs of a serious spine problem.

A scan such as an MRI is not the right next step. In the first few weeks, scans in this situation usually don't change treatment, and they often show changes that are common in people without pain and can cause unnecessary worry. Australian guidance is to hold off on scans unless there are warning signs, or the pain isn't settling after about four to six weeks.

Most people with this kind of strain are much better within two to six weeks. The back heals best if you keep gently moving rather than resting on the couch all day. Short walks, changing position often, and getting back to normal activities as you can are better than bed rest. A heat pack is fine. Avoid heavy lifting and sudden twisting for now, but don't stay still.
