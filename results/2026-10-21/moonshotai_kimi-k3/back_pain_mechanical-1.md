DR: Daniel, here's the honest picture on the MRI. Your story and my examination show a straightforward muscle/joint strain with no sign of a slipped disc pressing on a nerve and none of the danger signs we screen for. In that situation, national guidelines actually recommend AGAINST scanning, for three reasons:

1. It won't change what we do — the treatment is the same whatever the scan shows, because the treatment is time, movement and pain relief.
2. Scans of healthy 41-year-olds almost always show "wear and tear" changes that have nothing to do with the pain, and those findings often cause unnecessary worry and sometimes lead to treatments people don't need.
3. It keeps the scan in reserve for when it would actually matter.

If things aren't improving over the next few weeks, or you develop leg pain, numbness or weakness, then a scan becomes appropriate and I'll arrange it promptly.

DR: That's a very fair question, and a common worry.

Here's how we can be confident: a "slipped disc" only becomes a surgical problem when it presses hard on a nerve — and that produces a very specific pattern: pain shooting down the leg below the knee, numbness, and weakness. You've just had a thorough nerve examination and every single one of those checks was normal: full strength in both legs, normal reflexes, normal sensation, and the tests that stretch the nerves didn't reproduce any leg pain. So there is no sign of nerve compression today.

And even for people who DO get a disc pressing on a nerve, the great majority — around 8 or 9 out of 10 — improve without surgery over a few months, because the body reabsorbs the disc material. Surgery is reserved for a small minority with severe, persistent nerve problems or the emergency bladder symptoms I mentioned.

What you have — pain staying in the back and buttock, worse with certain movements, better with rest and heat, normal nerves — is the classic pattern of a simple strain, and these heal with time and movement. The best thing you can do to protect your back long-term is exactly what we've discussed: stay active now, build back to regular exercise, strengthen your core and legs, and lift with your legs rather than your back next time there's a pot plant to move.
