DR: Daniel, here's what I've found. Your examination is reassuring. The pain and tenderness are in the muscles beside your spine on the left, with some muscle spasm, and your spine bones themselves aren't tender. Your legs are completely normal: strength, reflexes, sensation and the nerve stretch tests are all fine. Put together with the story – a lifting strain, pain that's worse with movement and better with rest, and no warning signs like fever, weight loss, leg symptoms or bladder or bowel problems – this is a mechanical low back strain, which is very common and very much expected to get better over the next couple of weeks.

About the MRI: I understand the worry about slipped discs, but I'd actually recommend against a scan right now, and here's why. Scans aren't needed for back pain like yours unless there are warning signs or nerve problems, and you have none. Also, MRIs very often show "disc bulges" in people who have no back pain at all – they're like grey hairs on a scan – and seeing them tends to make people more worried and more likely to have unnecessary treatment, without changing recovery. Even if a disc were slightly irritated, the treatment at this stage would be exactly the same. If you aren't improving in about 4 to 6 weeks, or any new symptoms appear, I'd absolutely reconsider imaging then.

For treatment: I've prescribed an anti-inflammatory, ibuprofen 400 mg three times a day with food for up to 5 days, which usually works better than paracetamol for this. You can keep taking paracetamol as well. I've deliberately avoided strong painkillers like codeine – they don't work better for back pain and have more side effects, and I've noted you reacted to tramadol. Heat packs are good – keep using them. The most important thing is to keep moving: gentle walking several times a day, changing positions regularly, and avoiding lying on the couch for long stretches, which actually slows recovery. Bed rest is the one thing we know makes back pain last longer. Try to return to normal activities and work as you can, even if it's a bit uncomfortable – hurt doesn't mean harm here. A desk set-up at a proper table rather than the couch would help. I'd also suggest seeing a physiotherapist for some guidance on movement and exercises, which I can arrange.

Come back or seek help urgently if you develop any numbness or weakness in your legs, numbness around your back passage or genitals, difficulty passing urine or losing control of your bladder or bowels, fever, or pain that becomes constant and severe at night. Otherwise, I'd like to see you again in about two weeks to check on progress. Does that all make sense, and how do you feel about holding off on the scan?
