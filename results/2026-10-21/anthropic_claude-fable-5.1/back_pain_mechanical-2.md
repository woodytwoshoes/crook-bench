DR: Daniel, here's what I've found and what I recommend.

Your story and my examination fit a simple muscular strain of the lower back — the muscles beside your spine on the left are tight and tender, your spine bones themselves aren't tender, and your legs are completely normal: full strength, normal reflexes and sensation, and the nerve stretch tests are negative. There are no warning signs of a trapped nerve, infection, or anything else serious. The pain going into your buttock is very common with muscle strain and doesn't mean a disc problem.

About the MRI: I know you'd like one, but I'd actually advise against it right now, and here's why. Scans aren't recommended for back pain like yours in the first 6 weeks unless there are warning signs, which you don't have. MRI scans of people your age very often show "disc bulges" and wear and tear that are completely normal and painless — finding those tends to cause worry and sometimes unnecessary treatment, without changing what we do. If your pain hasn't settled in about 6 weeks, or if any warning signs develop, we will absolutely look at imaging then. Most people with this type of injury are much better in 1–2 weeks and recovered within 6 weeks.

The plan:
1. Keep moving. Lying on the couch feels better in the moment, but resting too much actually slows recovery and stiffens things up. Gentle walking several times a day, gradually increasing, and short periods sitting rather than lying for long stretches. Avoid heavy lifting for now but don't avoid normal activity.
2. Heat packs are fine and helpful — keep using them.
3. Medicine: I've prescribed ibuprofen 400 mg three times a day with food for up to 5 days — an anti-inflammatory that works better than paracetamol for this. You can take paracetamol alongside it, 1 g up to four times a day, for the first few days. Stop the ibuprofen if you get stomach pain, black stools or indigestion. I won't be using tramadol or other strong painkillers — they don't help back pain much and you've reacted badly before.
4. I've referred you to a physiotherapist, who can help with exercises and getting you moving confidently again.
5. Work: since you're desk-based, continuing to work from home is reasonable — just get up and move every 30 minutes. I can give you a certificate if you need one.

Please come back urgently or go to the emergency department straight away if you develop: numbness around your back passage or genitals, trouble passing urine or any loss of bowel or bladder control, weakness or numbness in a leg, pain spreading down the leg below the knee, fever, or pain that becomes severe and constant at night.
