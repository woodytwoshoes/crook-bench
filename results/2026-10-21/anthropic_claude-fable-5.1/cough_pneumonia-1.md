DR: Mark, here's what's going on. Your examination and the chest X-ray show you have pneumonia — a bacterial infection in the lower part of your right lung. That explains the fever, shaking chills, rusty-coloured phlegm, and the sharp pain when you breathe in, which is the lining of the lung being irritated. The good news is that your oxygen level, blood pressure and pulse are all reasonable, you're alert and drinking well, and you're otherwise healthy, so this is safe to treat at home with tablets rather than in hospital.

The plan:
1. Antibiotics: amoxicillin, 2 capsules (1 gram) three times a day for 7 days. Take the first dose as soon as you get them. Finish the whole course even when you feel better.
2. Paracetamol, 2 tablets up to four times a day (no more than 8 tablets in 24 hours) for the fever and the chest pain — it'll also make it easier to take deep breaths and cough the phlegm up, which helps the lung clear.
3. Rest, keep drinking plenty of fluids, and stay off work — you'll need at least this week off, and I'll give you a medical certificate. Please don't go back to the classroom until the fever has settled and you're feeling stronger.
4. I've sent off blood tests, a phlegm sample and a nose swab for viruses including COVID and flu. I'll call you with results in a day or two.
5. Review: I want to see you again in 48 hours — the day after tomorrow — to check you're improving. You should notice the fever easing within 2 to 3 days. If it hasn't, we may need to change the antibiotic.
